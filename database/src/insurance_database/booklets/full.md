# Example Auto Insurance: Full coverage policy booklet

## About this booklet

This booklet is an illustration made for a software demo. It is not real insurance, and Example Auto Insurance is not a real company. Real auto policies are longer and differ from state to state.

This booklet explains the Full coverage level. It covers damage and injuries you cause to other people, damage to your own car in a collision, and damage to your own car from almost anything else, such as theft, fire, vandalism, weather, animals and broken glass. Your declarations page (the summary page of your policy) shows your policy number, your car, your listed drivers and your deductible.

## Words used in this booklet

- **You:** the policyholder named on the declarations page.
- **Your car:** the vehicle shown on the declarations page.
- **Listed driver:** a driver named on the declarations page.
- **Limit:** the most we pay for one accident, for one kind of loss.
- **Deductible:** the amount you pay toward a claim for your own car before we pay. It's shown on your declarations page. Liability claims have no deductible.
- **Actual cash value:** what your car was worth just before the loss, given its age, mileage and condition.
- **Collision:** your car hitting, or being hit by, another vehicle or an object, or rolling over.
- **Comprehensive:** cover for damage to your car that isn't a collision.
- **Third party:** anyone other than you and your listed drivers.

## What Liability covers

Liability coverage pays for harm you cause to others while driving your car. If you or a listed driver causes an accident, we pay for:

- Damage to other people's property, such as their car, a fence, a storefront or a mailbox.
- Injuries to other people, including their medical bills and lost wages.
- Legal costs if someone takes you to court over an accident we cover.

We only pay when the driver of your car is legally responsible for the accident, in full or in part. If the accident was shared fault, we pay your share.

## Your limits

Every policy has these Liability limits for each accident:

- Injuries to other people: up to $50,000 for each person and $100,000 in total.
- Damage to other people's property: up to $50,000 in total.

These are sometimes written as 50/100/50. If the cost of an accident is higher than your limits, you may have to pay the rest yourself. Legal costs are paid on top of the limits.

Collision and comprehensive have no fixed limit: the most we pay is your car's actual cash value, less your deductible.

## What collision covers

Collision coverage pays to repair your car after a collision, whoever was at fault. For example:

- You rear-end another car in traffic.
- Another driver backs into your car in a parking lot and drives off.
- You hit a pole, a wall, a curb or a guardrail.
- Your car rolls over.

You pay your deductible, and we pay the rest of the repair cost, up to your car's actual cash value.

If another driver was at fault, we may claim the cost back from their insurer. If we get it back, we refund your deductible.

## Theft

If your car is stolen, tell the police first, then tell us within 24 hours and give us the police report number.

- If your car isn't found within 30 days, we treat it as a total loss and pay you its actual cash value, less your deductible.
- If it's found damaged, we pay for the repairs, less your deductible.
- If someone breaks in or tries to steal it, we pay for the broken locks, windows and ignition.

Things inside your car, such as a laptop, a phone or tools, aren't covered by this policy. Your homeowners or renters insurance may cover them.

## Fire and vandalism

We pay to repair your car if it's damaged by fire, or damaged on purpose by someone else. Vandalism includes keyed or spray-painted paint, slashed tires, and smashed windows or mirrors. Report vandalism to the police and give us the report number. You pay your deductible.

## Weather damage

We pay to repair weather damage to your car, including:

- Hail dents and broken glass from hail.
- Flood and rising water, including water inside the engine or the car.
- Wind, and damage from falling trees, branches and other objects.
- Lightning.

You pay your deductible. Don't try to start a car that has been in a flood: it can wreck the engine, and we may not pay for damage caused by starting it.

## Hitting an animal

Hitting a deer or another animal is covered by comprehensive coverage, not collision. We pay to repair your car, less your deductible. Damage from animals inside the car, such as rodents chewing through wires, is also covered.

## Windshield and window glass

We pay to repair or replace your windshield and windows, however they were damaged, for example by a stone thrown up from the road.

- **Repairing a chip or small crack:** no deductible. You pay nothing.
- **Replacing the windshield or a window:** you pay your deductible, and we pay the rest.

A repair is quicker and keeps the original glass, so we repair when the damage is small enough.

## Towing

If your car can't be driven after a covered loss, we pay up to $100 each time to tow it to the nearest repair shop that can fix it.

## Rental car while your car is repaired

If your car can't be driven after a covered loss, or it's in the repair shop for a covered repair, we pay for a rental car:

- Up to $40 a day.
- For up to 30 days, or until the repair is finished, whichever comes first.

If your car is stolen, the rental car starts 48 hours after you report the theft to us, and stops when the car is found or we pay the claim.

We don't pay for fuel, extra insurance from the rental company, or a rental car while your car is being repaired for something this policy doesn't cover.

## When your car is a total loss

If repairing your car would cost more than it's worth, we call it a total loss. We pay you your car's actual cash value, less your deductible, and the car then belongs to us. If you still owe money on the car, we pay your lender first.

## Who is covered to drive

Your policy covers:

- You and every listed driver.
- Someone who borrows your car now and then, with your permission, if they hold a valid driver's license.

Anyone who lives with you and drives your car regularly must be added to the policy as a listed driver. If they are not listed, we may refuse a claim when they were driving.

## What Full coverage does not cover

Even Full coverage does not pay for:

- Injuries to you or your listed drivers.
- Things inside your car, such as a laptop, a phone or tools.
- Wear and tear, rust, or mechanical and electrical breakdowns, unless they're caused by a covered loss.
- Damage from starting a car that has been in a flood.
- Accidents while your car is used for paid rides or paid deliveries, such as ridesharing or food delivery apps.
- Racing, speed tests or driving on a track.
- Damage you, a listed driver or someone acting for you causes on purpose.
- Accidents while the car is driven by someone without a valid driver's license.

## How to make a claim

1. Make sure everyone is safe. Call 911 if anyone is hurt.
2. Swap names, phone numbers, insurance details and license plates with the other drivers. Take photos of the scene and the damage if it's safe to do so.
3. Report the claim to us as soon as you can, and within 30 days. Report a theft within 24 hours. Tell us the date, the place, what happened and who was involved.
4. Don't admit fault or agree to pay anyone at the scene. Let us handle it.

We give each claim a claim number and an adjuster. Use the claim number whenever you contact us.

## What happens after you claim

Your adjuster is the person at Example Auto Insurance who handles your claim. They:

- Ask you, the other people involved and any witnesses what happened.
- Decide, with you, who was at fault.
- Arrange an estimate of the damage to your car, and agree the repair cost with the repair shop.
- Deal with the other people's claims against you, and pay what your policy covers.
- Keep notes on the claim, and tell you when it's settled or if it's denied.

You can choose any licensed repair shop. If you use one of our approved repair shops, we pay them directly and guarantee their work for as long as you own the car.

A claim can be denied if the policy doesn't cover what happened, for example if your car was being used for food deliveries. Your adjuster will explain why in writing.
