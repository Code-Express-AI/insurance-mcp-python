# Example Auto Insurance: Liability policy booklet

## About this booklet

This booklet is an illustration made for a software demo. It is not real insurance, and Example Auto Insurance is not a real company. Real auto policies are longer and differ from state to state.

This booklet explains the Liability coverage level. It covers damage and injuries you cause to other people. It does not cover damage to your own car. Your declarations page (the summary page of your policy) shows your policy number, your car, your listed drivers and your deductible.

## Words used in this booklet

- **You:** the policyholder named on the declarations page.
- **Your car:** the vehicle shown on the declarations page.
- **Listed driver:** a driver named on the declarations page.
- **Limit:** the most we pay for one accident, for one kind of loss.
- **Deductible:** the amount you pay toward a claim before we pay. Liability claims have no deductible.
- **Third party:** anyone other than you and your listed drivers.

## What Liability covers

Liability coverage pays for harm you cause to others while driving your car. If you or a listed driver causes an accident, we pay for:

- Damage to other people's property, such as their car, a fence, a storefront or a mailbox.
- Injuries to other people, including their medical bills and lost wages.
- Legal costs if someone takes you to court over an accident we cover.

We only pay when the driver of your car is legally responsible for the accident, in full or in part. If the accident was shared fault, we pay your share.

## Your limits

Every Liability policy has these limits for each accident:

- Injuries to other people: up to $50,000 for each person and $100,000 in total.
- Damage to other people's property: up to $50,000 in total.

These are sometimes written as 50/100/50. If the cost of an accident is higher than your limits, you may have to pay the rest yourself. Legal costs are paid on top of the limits.

## Who is covered to drive

Your policy covers:

- You and every listed driver.
- Someone who borrows your car now and then, with your permission, if they hold a valid driver's license.

Anyone who lives with you and drives your car regularly must be added to the policy as a listed driver. If they are not listed, we may refuse a claim when they were driving.

## What Liability does not cover

Liability coverage does not pay for damage to your own car, whatever the cause. This includes:

- Damage to your car in a collision, even when the other driver was at fault. In that case, claim from the other driver's insurer.
- Theft of your car, or of things inside it.
- Fire, vandalism, hail, flood, falling trees or other weather damage.
- Windshield and window damage.
- A rental car while your car is being repaired.

We also do not pay for:

- Injuries to you or your listed drivers.
- Accidents while your car is used for paid rides or paid deliveries, such as ridesharing or food delivery apps.
- Racing, speed tests or driving on a track.
- Damage someone causes on purpose.
- Accidents while the car is driven by someone without a valid driver's license.

If you want cover for your own car, ask about our Liability + collision or Full coverage levels.

## How to make a claim

1. Make sure everyone is safe. Call 911 if anyone is hurt.
2. Swap names, phone numbers, insurance details and license plates with the other drivers. Take photos of the scene and the damage if it's safe to do so.
3. Report the accident to us as soon as you can, and within 30 days. Tell us the date, the place, what happened and who was involved.
4. Don't admit fault or agree to pay anyone at the scene. Let us handle it.

We give each claim a claim number and an adjuster. Use the claim number whenever you contact us.

## What happens after you claim

Your adjuster is the person at Example Auto Insurance who handles your claim. They:

- Ask you, the other people involved and any witnesses what happened.
- Decide, with you, who was at fault.
- Deal with the other people's claims against you, and pay what your policy covers.
- Keep notes on the claim, and tell you when it's settled or if it's denied.

A claim can be denied if the policy doesn't cover what happened, for example if the driver wasn't allowed to drive your car. Your adjuster will explain why in writing.
